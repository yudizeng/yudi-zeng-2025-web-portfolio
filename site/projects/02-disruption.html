02 / INTERDEPENDENCE · OPERATIONAL SYSTEM

From Space to System

How do product, place, people, and channels come together in a working independent venture?

As the sole operator, with no external funding, I transformed a former soil-testing lab in a residential development into SUS CLINIC, a fashion venture built around Remake with a complementary vintage retail line. I adapted the space, developed the offer, and connected sourcing, display, customer routes, and operations into a functioning commercial system.

My contribution: Spatial adaptation, product and brand strategy, material sourcing, partnership coordination, staff recruitment, and independent operation.Xiamen, China · Agreement signed 26 February 2024; renovation planned to finish 30 March and completed December 2024 · Opened 1 March 2025

Commercial systems / Market fit / Material circulation / Operations

BRAND STRATEGY / MARKET RESEARCH

Translating the offer for a wider fashion audience

I researched buyer shops across the Asia-Pacific region, visited Redress’s physical shop in Hong Kong, and explored potential partnerships with local shops, Shanghai-based buyers, and large Instagram accounts focused on Remake. These encounters helped me understand how different audiences recognized and valued the offer.

Remake remained the core product, but it reached a more specialized audience and was harder to convert into purchases; selling it often meant explaining circularity and the making process. Vintage was a more familiar entry point for customers already interested in fashion and nostalgia, and its audience overlapped with Remake. To reduce explanation friction, I presented the shop publicly as a vintage store while keeping Remake at the core. I led communication with design, material, and feeling, with circularity embedded in the product’s value. I used vintage customers’ willingness to pay as one reference when pricing Remake.
